Topology, geometry, and AI systems

The geometry and topology of learned representations.

I study how the structure of learned representations changes during training and fine-tuning, and how those changes relate to model behavior.

My work combines topological data analysis, statistical calibration, and efficient computation. I am interested in which distinctions a model retains, how they relate across a label hierarchy, and whether structural changes provide useful signals of behavioral deterioration.

Current projects connect controlled point-cloud experiments with the evaluation of language model representations and safety. Earlier work focused on optimal transport, knowledge graphs, and the alignment of embedding spaces.

Current directions

Which relations in a representation remain recoverable as a model changes, and when does that matter for behavior? I compare topological measurements with simpler spectral and geometric diagnostics, test their reliability under controlled changes, and measure their predictive value alongside their computational cost. Compression and structural change can accompany useful learning as well as degradation; the distinction needs empirical evidence.

Calibrated structural diagnostics

Using controlled point-cloud interventions to compare spectral, geometric, and persistent-homology diagnostics. The goal is to understand which changes each measurement detects, how reliably it does so, and when topology adds useful information.

calibration controlled benchmarks persistent homology

Hierarchical structure and safety

Following fixed safety-taxonomy prompts through language model fine-tuning. Inclusion-induced homology maps describe how child-category structure is retained in parents and domains; behavioral evaluation tests whether changes in those relationships are safety-relevant.

label hierarchies image persistence AI safety

Efficient temporal monitoring

Connecting representation motion to local topological updates, and studying the tradeoff between monitoring cost and signal fidelity. I evaluate whether structural measurements help anticipate later behavioral changes on independent training runs.

training trajectories incremental computation predictive validity

Past work

Past publications related to work in AI lecturing assistant design and AI/knowledge graph models applied to metal organic frameworks (MOFs) that arise in organic chemistry.

Knowledge graph - LLM synthesis of MOFs (metal organic frameworks)

MOFs are crystalline structures that can be applied to the capture of carbon and nuclear waste. Their synthesis procedures are written up in academic papers along with databases of structures and their properties. Sifting through all this material is a challenge for reticular chemists, therefore we introduced a unified knowledge graph that can ingest both structured databases and unstructured text, coupled with natural language search functionality and predictive linking.

KGQA4MAT

AI Lecture Assistants

Managing course syllabi, instructional materials, and lecture notes can be a headache for many professors, with minimal feedback from an increasingly shy student body. We introduced a knowledge graph to synthesize course materials, plot course maps, and integrate lecture recordings to identify points where the students lose engagement and recommend supporting exercises.
